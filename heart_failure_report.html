Univariate Biostatistical and Clinical Data Analysis of the Heart Failure Clinical Records Cohort

Executive Overview and Cohort Architecture

Heart failure represents a complex clinical syndrome arising from structural or functional cardiac abnormalities that impair the ability of the ventricles to fill with or eject blood at a rate commensurate with systemic metabolic requirements. Across global epidemiological registries, cardiovascular diseases account for approximately 17 million deaths annually, with congestive heart failure serving as a leading driver of hospitalization, progressive multi-organ dysfunction, and premature mortality (Chicco & Jurman, 2020). Quantitative characterization of empirical clinical cohorts through rigorous univariate biostatistics is essential prior to multivariable survival modeling, risk-score calibration, or machine learning deployment. Without comprehensive inspection of individual variable distributions, central tendencies, dispersion metrics, quartile boundaries, and tail behavior, downstream parametric estimators can be severely distorted by unaddressed skewness, extreme laboratory outliers, or retrospective single-value imputations.

The empirical dataset examined in this report, stored locally as heart_failure.csv and archived publicly in the UCI Machine Learning Repository Heart Failure Clinical Records Dataset, comprises 299 hospitalized patients diagnosed with heart failure due to left ventricular systolic dysfunction. The clinical records were originally collected between April and December 2015 at the Faisalabad Institute of Cardiology and the Allied Hospital in Faisalabad, Punjab, Pakistan, and first analyzed using Cox proportional hazards regression and Kaplan-Meier survival curves by Ahmad et al. (2017). All 299 individuals in the cohort had experienced prior heart failure decompensation placing them in functional Class III or Class IV under the New York Heart Association (NYHA) classification system, indicating marked limitation of physical activity or symptoms of cardiac insufficiency occurring at rest (Chicco & Jurman, 2020).

Each patient record in heart_failure.csv captures 13 clinical, demographic, hematologic, metabolic, and longitudinal follow-up variables. Seven of these variables are measured on a continuous or discrete numerical scale: age (age), creatinine phosphokinase (creatinine_phosphokinase), ejection fraction (ejectionfraction), platelets (platelets), serum creatinine (serum_creatinine), serum sodium (sodium), and follow-up time (time). The remaining six variables are measured on a dichotomous categorical scale: anemia (anemia), diabetes (diabetes), hypertension (hypertension), biological sex (sex), smoking status (smoking), and mortality outcome during follow-up (death). A comprehensive structural inventory of the 13 variables, their database identifiers, standardized clinical nomenclature, measurement scales, and physiological reference intervals is presented in Table 1.

Data Completeness, Mathematical Framework, and Retrospective Curation

An initial audit of heart_failure.csv confirms that all 299 rows and 13 columns contain populated entries, yielding exactly zero missing observations (0% missingness) at the file level across every numerical and categorical variable. However, close inspection of the empirical frequency distributions reveals an important structural property of the dataset: prior to public release, missing laboratory records in the hospital charts were populated using mean imputation for certain continuous biomarkers. Specifically, in the platelets column, the exact value of 263,358.03 kiloplatelets/mL appears 25 times (8.36% of the cohort), which corresponds precisely to the arithmetic sample mean of the remaining 274 non-imputed patient measurements (263,358.0292 kiloplatelets/mL). Similarly, in the creatinine phosphokinase column, the integer value of 582 mcg/L appears 47 times (15.72% of the cohort), matching the rounded arithmetic mean of the remaining 252 observations (581.8095 mcg/L). In the serum creatinine column, the values 1.83 mg/dL (8 occurrences) and 1.18 mg/dL (11 occurrences) correspond to subgroup means among deceased and surviving patients, respectively, as documented in the comparative tables of Chicco & Jurman (2020). Recognizing these point-mass concentrations is essential when interpreting parametric variance estimates and quartile boundaries.

For each of the seven numerical variables X = {x1, x2, …, x_n} with sample size n = 299, the univariate parametric and non-parametric summary statistics are defined as follows. The sample mean (x̄) measures the center of mass of the empirical distribution:

x̄ = (1 / n) ∑i=1n xi

The unbiased sample variance (s2) and sample standard deviation (s) quantify dispersion around the arithmetic mean using Bessel's correction with n - 1 = 298 degrees of freedom:

ss2 = [1 / (n − 1)] ∑i=1n (xi − x̄)2,   s = √(s2)

To characterize order statistics that remain robust against extreme skewness and heavy tails, the sample observations are sorted in ascending order x(1) ≤ x(2) ≤ … ≤ x(n), where the minimum is x(1) and the maximum is x(n). For any probability p ∈ {0.25, 0.50, 0.75}, the linear interpolation quantile estimator sets the virtual index h = 1 + p(n - 1). With n = 299, the median (p = 0.50) falls at exact integer rank h = 1 + 0.50(298) = 150, so Median = x(150). The first quartile (Q1, p = 0.25) falls at rank h = 1 + 0.25(298) = 75.5, midway between x(75) and x(76), while the third quartile (Q3, p = 0.75) falls at rank h = 1 + 0.75(298) = 224.5, midway between x(224) and x(225). In the box-and-whisker plots, the central box spans the interquartile range (IQR = Q3 - Q1), the horizontal line within the box marks the median, the whiskers extend to the most extreme observations within the inner Tukey fences [Q1 - 1.5 × IQR, Q3 + 1.5 × IQR], and observations beyond those fences are plotted individually as outliers.

For each of the six categorical variables C with unique category set K, the frequency fk counts the exact number of patients belonging to category k ∈ K, and the relative frequency pk expresses that count as a proportion of the non-missing sample size n = 299:

pkpk = fk / n,   Percentagek = 100 × pk%

From a pathophysiological perspective, creatine kinase catalyzes the reversible phosphorylation of creatine by adenosine triphosphate (ATP) in high-energy tissues—principally myocardium (CK-MB isoenzyme), skeletal muscle (CK-MM), and brain tissue (CK-BB). Anemia is the single most prevalent comorbidity in this heart failure cohort, affecting more than two out of every five hospitalized patients (43.14%). In chronic heart failure, anemia develops through a multifactorial interplay known as the cardiorenal-anemia syndrome: chronic renal insufficiency blunts endogenous erythropoietin synthesis in peritubular interstitial fibroblasts, systemic proinflammatory cytokines (such as interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha) upregulate hepatic hepcidin to sequester iron within reticuloendothelial macrophages and impair duodenal iron absorption, and chronic extracellular fluid expansion causes hemodilution. Reduced hemoglobin oxygen-carrying capacity forces a compensatory increase in myocardial workload and sympathetic tone, accelerating ventricular remodeling. Within this cohort, anemia was present in 47.92% (46 of 96) of patients who died compared to 40.89% (83 of 203) of patients who survived (Chicco & Jurman, 2020).

Conclusions and Clinical Synthesis

Synthesizing the univariate summary statistics and graphical visualizations across all 13 variables in heart_failure.csv yields five primary analytical and clinical conclusions:

1. Predominance of Systolic Dysfunction (HFrEF) with High Short-Term Mortality: The cohort represents a severely ill tertiary heart failure population (NYHA Class III–IV) in which both the mean (38.0836%) and median (38.0000%) left ventricular ejection fraction lie below the 40% threshold defining Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF), and 75% of all patients have an ejection fraction at or below 45.0000%. Despite a relatively young median age of 60.0000 years (mean 60.8339 years, range 40.0000–95.0000 years), nearly one-third of the cohort (32.11%, n = 96) died during the follow-up period (mean 130.2609 days, median 115.0000 days), underscoring the rapid clinical deterioration associated with decompensated systolic dysfunction.

2. Divergence Between Parametric and Non-Parametric Summaries in Skewed Biomarkers: Across the seven numerical variables, distributional geometry falls into three distinct regimes. First, age (g1 = +0.4231), ejection fraction (g1 = +0.5554), and follow-up time (g1 = +0.1278) exhibit relatively symmetric or moderately skewed distributions with zero or minimal outliers (0, 2, and 0 outliers, respectively), where the sample mean and median agree closely. Second, creatinine phosphokinase (mean 581.8395 mcg/L vs. median 250.0000 mcg/L, g1 = +4.4631, 29 high outliers up to 7,861.0000 mcg/L) and serum creatinine (mean 1.3939 mg/dL vs. median 1.1000 mg/dL, g1 = +4.4560, 29 high outliers up to 9.4000 mg/dL) exhibit extreme positive skewness and leptokurtosis (g2 > 25). For both biomarkers, acute myocardial injury and severe renal dysfunction pull the sample mean up to the 75th percentile (Q3 = 582.0000 mcg/L for CPK and Q3 = 1.4000 mg/dL for serum creatinine), rendering the median and interquartile range far more faithful summaries of typical patient values than the mean and variance. Third, serum sodium is negatively skewed (g1 = -1.0481) with 4 severe lower outliers down to 113.0000 mEq/L, while platelets exhibit bilateral outliers (7 thrombocytopenic outliers down to 25,100.0000 kiloplatelets/mL and 14 thrombocytotic outliers up to 850,000.0000 kiloplatelets/mL).

3. Cardiorenal-Hematologic Comorbidity Triad: The univariate distributions highlight the tight clinical intersection of cardiac pump failure, renal impairment, dilutional hyponatremia, and anemia. More than one-quarter of patients exhibit clinical renal dysfunction (Q3 = 1.4000 mg/dL for serum creatinine, with 29 outliers ≥ 2.20 mg/dL), over one-quarter exhibit clinical hyponatremia (Q1 = 134.0000 mEq/L for serum sodium, below the 135 mEq/L lower limit of normal), and 43.14% (n = 129) suffer from clinical anemia (hematocrit < 36%). Alongside high rates of diabetes (41.81%, n = 125), hypertension (35.12%, n = 105), and male predominance (64.88%, n = 194), these findings demonstrate that advanced heart failure in this population is a systemic multi-organ syndrome rather than an isolated hemodynamic disorder.

4. Detection of Retrospective Mean Imputation Artifacts: While file-level inspection shows zero missing entries across all 13 columns, distributional frequency analysis reveals that missing clinical laboratory values in the original hospital charts were populated via mean imputation prior to public release (specifically 47 entries of 582 mcg/L in creatinine phosphokinase and 25 entries of 263,358.03 kiloplatelets/mL in platelets, as well as subgroup mean values in serum creatinine). Analysts building downstream regression or machine learning pipelines on heart_failure.csv should account for these point-mass concentrations, as single-value mean imputation artificially compresses sample variance and can create synthetic split thresholds in tree-based classifiers.

5. Implications for Clinical Risk Stratification: Because ejection fraction and serum creatinine capture the two primary physiological axes of cardiorenal failure—left ventricular systolic output and glomerular filtration clearance—their distributions provide the highest prognostic separation between surviving and deceased patients, whereas follow-up time reflects the temporal survival window itself. Together, combining parametric (mean, variance, standard deviation) and non-parametric (minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum, and box-and-whisker outlier fences) univariate diagnostics establishes a rigorous empirical foundation for clinical decision support.
